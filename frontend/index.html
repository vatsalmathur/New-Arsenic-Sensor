What it is

A small catamaran-style surface robot (1 m long, 8-20 kg) that drives itself across a lake, creek, or reservoir. At each waypoint, it pulls water into a sealed internal chamber, screens it for arsenic, and tags the reading with GPS.

The sensor never touches open water to avoid fouling. Instead, the robot brings a filtered, temperature-controlled sample to the sensor, ensuring repeatable results.

Screening Tool, not a certified lab instrument

The Problem it Solves

Arsenic has no colour, smell, or taste. The WHO provisional guideline is 10 µg/L. You cannot find it without an instrument, and stationary lab instruments are incredibly expensive.

The gap is not sensitivity, it is mobility. Today, mapping a water body means collecting bottles by hand, which is slow and expensive.

AquaSentry closes that gap in three ways:

  • 1
    Coverage
    A grid survey produces a contamination contour map, not a single reading.
  • 2
    Cost per sample
    Once built, each additional reading costs only reagents and time, not a lab fee and a courier.
  • 3
    Context
    Every arsenic value is logged with its location and supporting water-quality measurements.

What it Achieves

Direct output: A geotagged arsenic contour map of a water body, with pH, conductivity, turbidity, and temperature logged at every point.

The impact: You stop guessing which wells or ponds to test, and start testing the ones the map says are worth testing. Lab capacity goes to the samples that matter.
